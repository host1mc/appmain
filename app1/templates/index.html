Questions, answered straight

Wondering how the free service works? Here's exactly how.

Is it really free, or free for now?

Free for life, funded by ads. Free accounts stay live with a one-click weekly renewal, and no paid tier ever unlocks. Hosting a status bot costs us very little per server, and a bigger Discord community is worth more to us than subscription revenue.

What's the catch?

The site runs ads, and we'd like you in the Discord. That's it. Your bot isn't rate-limited, badged or downgraded, and we don't upsell you inside it.

Is my bot token safe?

Your token is encrypted before it's stored and only ever decrypted by the hosting engine that needs it. It's never sent to the browser and never shown back to you after you save it.

Does it work with Bedrock as well as Java?

Both. Player count, version, MOTD and the player list are pulled for Java and Bedrock servers alike, and the embed refreshes automatically.

What happens when my Minecraft server goes down?

The embed says so, honestly and in real time. It flips to offline rather than freezing on a stale player count, so your members can trust what it shows.

How often does the status refresh?

You set the interval in the builder. The minimum is 15 seconds so we do not hammer your Minecraft server or Discord's API. Most people leave it at 60 seconds.

Do I need to keep a computer on?

No. Once hosting is started, our engine polls your server and edits the Discord message. Closing the browser does not stop the embed.

What Discord permissions does the bot need?

View Channel, Send Messages, and Read Message History on the status channel. It posts one embed and then edits that same message. It does not need administrator, kick, or ban.

How do I delete my account?

You can delete your account from the panel. That stops your bots and removes the account data we hold. The full list of what we store is in the Privacy Policy.

How setup actually works

A short walkthrough so you know what you are signing up for — not a sales pitch.

1. Create a Discord bot

In the Discord Developer Portal, create an application, open the Bot tab, and copy the token. Invite that bot to your server with permission to view the status channel, send messages, and read message history. You paste the token into our panel once; it is encrypted before it is stored and is never shown back to you.

2. Point it at your Minecraft server

Enter the public IP or hostname and the port. Java Edition uses 25565 by default; Bedrock uses 19132. We query the public status endpoint only — we never need RCON, FTP, or a plugin on your server.

3. Choose the Discord channel

Paste the guild ID and the channel ID. The first time the engine runs it posts one embed. After that it edits that same message on your refresh interval, so the channel stays a single live card instead of a spam of status posts.

4. Design the card, then start hosting

Colours, title, fields, player-list size, and the wording for online and offline are yours. Save, then start hosting. The engine picks the bot up on the next tick. The full walkthrough — Developer Portal, invite URL, IDs — is on the help page.
